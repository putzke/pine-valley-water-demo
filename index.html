Iron and Beaver Counties, Utah

Reliable water for Cedar City, Enoch and Kanarraville for the next 50 years and beyond.

The Pine Valley Water Supply Project will bring the District's existing water rights from Pine Valley to the Cedar City Valley, alongside conservation, recharge and reuse. Here is what is planned, what it costs, how the aquifer will be protected, and how you can be part of it.

Why the valley needs new water

The Cedar City Valley aquifer is pumped faster than nature refills it. The State's Groundwater Management Plan will phase pumping back to a safe level. Population is growing at the same time.

Conservation comes first, but it cannot do the whole job

The Record of Decision found that conservation alone cannot close the gap: per-person use would have to fall to between 62 and 112 gallons a day, depending on growth. The District is already doing its part.

  • 2,000+ acresof irrigation pivots converted to low-pressure systems
  • 7 rechargefacilities adding more than 14,000 acre-feet since 2016
  • Reusetreated-effluent project in preparation with Cedar City and Enoch
  • Freehome irrigation audits and Localscapes classes

Source: District community Q&A, December 2021. Figures should be refreshed with the District before publication.

Where the pipeline would go

Water would travel from wells in Pine Valley in Beaver County, south through BLM, State trust and private land, to the Cedar City Valley. Choose a category to see what the route crosses and who we coordinate with.

What the route has to cross

  • BLM land. Federal right-of-way grants are approved: a 50-foot permanent width plus a 70-foot temporary construction width.
  • State trust land. About 4 to 5 easements across School and Institutional Trust Lands (SITLA) parcels.
  • Private land. About 45 to 50 parcels will need easements. Each landowner gets one named contact. See the process.
  • Union Pacific Railroad. Two bored crossings, so trains are not interrupted.
  • SR-56. A bored crossing under the highway, permitted through UDOT.
  • County and city roads. Iron and Beaver County roads and Cedar City streets, with traffic control plans and restoration standards.
  • Kern River gas corridor. A high-pressure natural gas line. Crossings follow the operator's clearance and potholing requirements.
  • Power and communications. Rocky Mountain Power and other lines are located before work begins.
  • Other water and drainage. Washes and drainage channels are crossed under State Stream Channel Alteration permits.

Sources: BLM ROD; project permit matrix. Counts are current estimates and will change as design advances.

How this gets paid for

The District has said no single source will carry the project. The plan is to stack the lowest-cost sources first so that less falls on monthly water bills. Final numbers will be published here as each cost checkpoint is reached.

  1. 1
    Grants

    Federal and state grants carry no repayment.

  2. 2
    State revolving fund loans

    Low-interest loans for drinking water facilities.

  3. 3
    Federal WIFIA loan

    Long repayment terms for large, long-lived assets.

  4. 4
    Impact fees

    New growth pays for the capacity it uses.

  5. 5
    District funds and local partners

    Matching funds and early work, with member cities.

  6. 6
    Revenue bonds

    Used only for any remaining gap.

What will this mean for my bill?

In 2021 the District estimated that if only current water users repaid the project, it would add roughly $45 to $60 a month. A detailed financial plan was still in progress. Rate increases are aimed at upper tiers, so indoor use stays at lower rates while heavy lawn watering costs more.

Source: District community Q&A, December 2021. A current figure will replace this when the financial plan is complete.

Springs and senior water rights

The District has said it will avoid impacts to senior water right holders, including spring flows, and that senior rights would be made whole. Monitoring commitments are described in the Final EIS.

Wildlife and habitat

Parts of the route cross greater sage-grouse habitat and Utah prairie dog areas. Seasonal work limits and survey requirements will be posted before construction starts in each area.

Cultural resources and tribes

The BLM completed tribal and State Historic Preservation Office consultation. The District has said it is willing to meet with tribes directly, and we would keep that channel open throughout design and construction.

If the pipeline crosses your property

You will hear from us first, in person or by phone, before a survey crew or a letter arrives at the gate. This is how the process would work.

  1. Your named contact

    A letter and a call introduce one person who will be your point of contact throughout.

  2. Survey access, by agreement

    We ask permission, agree a time, and tell you what the crew will measure.

  3. Walk the route together

    We listen to concerns about gates, fences, crops, water and access, and share them with the design team.

  4. Easement documents explained

    Plain-language summary and a map of exactly what is being asked. The District's attorney handles terms.

  5. Construction notice

    Advance notice of timing, access and restoration plans, and a number that is answered.

  6. Restoration follow-up

    We return after construction to confirm the land is put back as promised.
